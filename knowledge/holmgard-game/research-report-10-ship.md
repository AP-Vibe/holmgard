## 1. Question

What vessel, crew and operating math should the game canon use for (a) the Baltic leg Birka → Aldeigjuborg, and (b) the eastern river legs beyond? What can be claimed as documented vs design space?

## 2. Documentary evidence — DAI ch. 9 (c. 950, Jenkins trans.)

The load-bearing primary text for the eastern trade's boat culture:

- **Monoxyla**: the Rus come to Constantinople in monoxyla ("single-straked boats", probably to be understood as very large dugouts) from Novgorod, Smolensk, Teliutza, Chernigov, Vyshegrad, collected at Kiev.
- **Boat supply chain**: Slav tributaries (Krivichians, Lenzanenes) cut the monoxyla in the winter mountains, float them out at spring thaw, and **sell them unfinished at Kiev**, where the Rus finish them and "furnish them with oars and rowlocks and other tackle from their old monoxyla, which they dismantle". Hull + tackle reuse is in the text.
- **Season clock**: finish at Kiev, depart June, muster 2–3 days at Vitichev, descend together. November → poliudia (tribute rounds); April → back to Kiev.
- **Portage mechanics (seven barrages)**: men wade the boats through the edges poling and feeling with their feet (barrages 1–6, as able); at the big fourth barrage, Aeifor, they land prow-first, post watchers against the Pechenegs, **walk the chained slaves six miles**, and "partly dragging their monoxyla, partly portaging them on their shoulders" get the boats across, reload and sail on.
- **Re-rig at St. Aitherios**: sails, masts and rudders are carried with them and re-fitted before the open-sea leg. Fully demountable rig.
- **Bibliographic note (Waugh)**: monoxyla read as "very large dugouts" — the band is wide; nothing in DAI gives tonnage, cargo weight, or crew per hull for trade.

**Provenance labels**: DOCUMENTED for mid-10th-c. practice; RECONSTRUCTED when applied to 911/912 (our setting is ~40 years earlier than Constantine's informants).

## 4. Experimental voyages (Edberg 2009, "Experimental 'Viking voyages' on Eastern European rivers 1983–2006", Situne Dei)

Full text archived at `research/sources/edberg-2009-experimental-voyages.md`. The operational record:

| Expedition | Boat | Route & result |
|---|---|---|
| **Krampmacken** 1983/85 (Nylén) | 8 m, Gotlandic find | Vistula upstream ~10 km/day rowing/towing, "despite much toil"; farther progress impossible; cart+wheels across the Carpathians (Dukla pass); Ondava→Bodrog→Tisza→Danube→Istanbul. No engines; provisions car alongside. |
| **Havørn** 1992 (Engøy) | 16 m, 2/3-scale Gokstad | Daugava: reduced to rowing/towing; 300 m of 8-knot rapids took **three days**; trucks around dams/watershed. "Shattered the stereotype that eastward voyages with this type of vessel had been practical." |
| **Aifur** 1994/96/01 (Johansson, Edberg) | 9.5 m mixed find | See §5. |
| **Heimlösa Rus** 2000 (Koivusalo) | 12 m Lapuri replica | Don upstream → Volga–Don canal → Volgograd → Astrakhan. (Locks and canal = modern assists on the Khazarian Way leg.) |
| **Himingläva** 2004 (Altrock) | 9.75 m, Gokstad small-boat | Dnieper mouth → Caucasus → Caspian goal (Ingvar's route); oxen over the 1150 m pass; car ferry across the Black Sea leg. |
| **Welet** 2006 (Wolski) | 12 m, Gdansk find | Vistula–Bug–(border)–Dniester; locks (Wloclawek dam), land transport; Bug low water forced truck across border. |

Edberg's general findings (his text): travelling on rivers against strong current or in shallow water is "challenging and sometimes quite impossible"; **"the notion that Scandinavians brought their own vessels to and across Russia ... is neither supported by archaeological and historical research, nor by these experiments"**; each river differs radically; water table differs year to year and season to season; keeled ships are heavy to handle and land transport is never easy; river travel is time-consuming even when viable; the Lovat's middle/upper reaches (~540 km) were not viable for boat travel, so the chronicle's "route along the Lovat" should be read as travellers **following the river** using sailable sections and completing on foot/horseback/sleigh; Sindbæk's transshipment-node reading (Staraya Ladoga, Rurikovo: nodes where sea/river/land transport changes, sledges in Novgorod) is the confirmed research frame; seagoing ships were left among countrymen for the winter where needed (Sorokin 2006).

## 5. The Aifur operational record (our canon hull)

Boat: **9.5 m long, 2.2 m wide, hull ~800 kg, 19 m² sail**, combining Baltic Viking-Age boat finds. Owned by a **ten-member association**; sailed with a sequence of crews of **about nine people** per leg.

Voyages: 1994 Sigtuna (Mälaren) → Baltic → Neva → Ladoga → Volkhov → **Novgorod: 1,382 km**. 1996 Novgorod → (Lovat impassable at low water from Kholm up) → Usvyatya/Dvina/Kasplya, second watershed crossed **on wheels made on site** → Dnieper: 1,568 km, ending at the Dnieper mouth (Kherson). Combined: **2,950 km in 113 effective days ≈ 26 km/day**.

Sailing profile: good before the wind on open water (Baltic, Ladoga, Ilmen, Dnieper); tacking only "to a certain degree"; rowing and sometimes sailing downstream works; adverse shallow current (Lovat) exceptionally demanding.

Key findings: only **very light vessels** suit the northern passage; upper Lovat and Kasplya navigable **only after snowy winters, for a short spring window**; Miklyayev's observation that period settlements sit metres below present river levels → Viking-Age water table was LOWER → rivers less navigable, and the passage may partly have run in **winter** (horse and sledge on frozen rivers and marshes); modern maps alone (Elmér's pre-Krampmacken study) grossly overestimate viability; hydro data for the Volkhov was publishable only via a hydrologist in Novgorod.

## 6. Atlantic capability (the "America" comparanda)

- **Snorri** (Carter, 1998): 54-foot knarr replica, crew of nine, Greenland → L'Anse aux Meadows. Finding: the knarr **cannot sail to windward**.
- **Draken Harald Hårfagre** (2016): modern-built longest longship, Haugesund → Newfoundland in ~6 weeks, via the old Iceland–Greenland waystation route, heavy storms en route (modern hybrid design, not a strict replica).
- Purpose here: the ocean-capability bound that justifies the knarr as the Baltic-leg canon.

## 7. Season/wintering verdict

Nationalmuseet of Denmark: a boat trip from Central Sweden to the Black Sea **and back** could not be completed before the Russian rivers freeze; every journey beyond Staraya Ladoga involved staying at least one Russian winter. With Miklyayev/Sindbæk (winter routes, transshipment nodes, sleigh transport) this triangulates three independent ways: the one-way structure and the winter-abroad consequence are evidentially solid, and winter itself is a transport medium (sledge legs), not just a fail state.

## 8. Design implications for Holmgard [CANON-LOCKED where marked]

1. **Vessel canon [CANON-LOCKED Nov 23, Andrzej: "lock it at Aifur replica"]**: Aifur-class small boat — 9.5 m, ~800 kg hull, 19 m² sail — for the eastern legs; knarr (Skuldelev-1-class) for the open Baltic leg. The two-size canon (Baltic knarr / eastern lodya) is now locked.
2. **Crew canon [CANON-LOCKED]**: ship's company of ten = skipper + five named crew + four hired keel-hands; ten rations/day (retuned and regression-verified in prototype 0.1: careful policy full-settlement d19–24 across 5 seeds, anchor 777 = full d22; neglect = scales 20 with Halfr lost; 4,249 assertions green). Aifur's crew-of-nine-per-leg plus a skipper figure is the direct experimental support.
3. **Hull-switching**: our own vessel does not continue beyond Ladoga; the ship itself becomes a market decision there (buy/fit a local lodya or monoxylon) — DAI (Kiev hull market + tackle from old boats), Edberg (Scandinavians did not bring their own vessels across Russia), Sorokin (left among countrymen for winter).
4. **Water-level gates**: river legs carry a seasonal viability gate (snowy-winter → short spring window on shallow upland rivers; summer drought = grounding risk). The Aifur's Lovat failure is the proof-of-concept; gates should be a leg-2 mechanic, harsher on upland rivers (Lovat class) than big lowland rivers (Dnieper class).
5. **Portage mechanics**: three modalities straight from the sources — wade/pole through the shallows, drag/shoulder across the barrage (crew fatigue + watcher vs raid risk, per DAI Aeifor), wheels/oxen for long land stages (Krampmacken, Himingläva). Cargo moves separately from the boat at the big portages (captives walked, in chains — DAI).
6. **Captive capacity** (design space, not attested): 0.8–1.5 m² deck footprint per captive from Run 8's manifest; same ration accounting as crew; canon band ~5–12 captives for a 10-man company on an Aifur-class hull. [PARKED for the slave-trade tone pass.]
7. **Art QC additions [CANON per Andrzej Nov 23]**: (a) ship size consistent within each leg's canon (knarr vs lodya sets); (b) realistic crew counts — never 2 sprites on a working deck; the 0.1 test set's 2–3 sprites are grandfathered for the prototype only; (c) NO "below-deck" anywhere — Viking craft are open boats, crew occluded by gunwale/rigging/cargo tarp, cargo covered by tarpaulin.
8. **Travel math**: ~26 km/day effective average (Aifur, mixed water/land); upriver towing ~10 km/day (Krampmacken); these are the pacing knobs for the day-clock of future legs; the 0.1 Birka→Aldeigjuborg one-day-per-beat model (~20 days) is compatible with the Aifur's real Sigtuna→Novgorod timing on comparable water.
