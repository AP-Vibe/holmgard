## Perplexity Run 1 — Oregon Trail-like games: mechanics, art, length, market (Nov 19, 2026)

Source: Andrzej's deep-research paste (49-product inventory, 15 dossiers, Parts 1–5 with synthesis). Quality high: frame-neutral spread honored, failures included, dev-claims vs evidence distinguished, per-prompt structure followed exactly.

### Findings (design-relevant)
- **Oregon Trail 1985 structural data (Bouchard, primary source)**: 18 landmarks available / ~16 visited, 15 segments; 6 forts; 4 river crossings (diminishing returns after 3–4); named party + persistent health/recovery = cheap emotional stakes; difficulty tuned so players fail runs 1–2 but believe they can improve; richer model than presentation (2 crossing animations for many outcomes); guide risk-reduction raised 50%→80% because worthwhile-but-small bonuses feel like scams.
- **Closest production comp: The Caribbean Sail** — solo dev maritime OT descendant, $14.99, 93% positive / 504 reviews, named sailors, wound→infection→amputation cascades, free expansions. The economical-representation proof.
- **Newest near-identical comp: The Göktürk: Caravan (Sep 22, 2026, $3.99)** — 7th-c. Silk Road caravan roguelite, 200 encounters / 50 goods / 12 cities advertised, AI-assisted art disclosed, merchant House persists past master death, toll-payment-reputation mechanic (paying raiders escalates future demands). Only 7 reviews — unvalidated, watch it.
- **THE WARNING CASE: The Great Whale Road** — closest theme comp (early-medieval North Sea, crew/ship/trading stops/seasonal voyages) = documented commercial failure, studio closed, sales stopped (Mar 2018). Cause: repetitive interchangeable stops, samey encounters, unclear systems. Attractive art + right theme ≠ sales.
- **Other failures/cautions**: The Long Journey Home (57%, maintenance grind crowds out discovery; dev-estimated 4–6h successful run but 49h to first success), Nowhere Prophet (long runs magnify unavoidable losses), Overland (75%, praised art, criticized chores), Silk (16 reviews, scale without refinement).
- **Success data**: Curious Expedition 217,816 units / $1.83M gross 2014–2020 (quiet launch: 61 copies day one of paid alpha); Sunless Sea 54,210 copies first 31 days / 100k by Feb 2015 (existing audience + crowdfunding + EA); Organ Trail $4.99/89%; FTL $9.99/95%/47k reviews. Price bands work at $5–$30; no single correct price.
- **Design mechanics bank**: trading-as-survival = 80 Days (cargo displaces provisions; money buys time), FTL (repair-now vs capability-later shared currency), Banner Saga (renown buys supplies AND hero upgrades; morale→performance), Pale Beyond (loyalty ≠ morale ≠ deployability, three separate concepts), Nowhere Prophet (wounds accumulate before permanent loss — injury changes the next leg), Lost Expedition (resource conversion without economy), Oregon Trail toll road vs rafting (toll = different risk profile, not just money).
- **Review-driven rules**: random maps don't prevent content familiarity (vary context/cost/consequence); hidden-knowledge checks feel unfair (show factors); recovery must change the story not repeat a chore; save systems must understand slow resource erosion; beautiful art doesn't redeem friction; long runs need proportionate catastrophe + warning time.
- **Pacing hypotheses (researcher's, not norms)**: 90–150 min successful run, 12–16 legs, 4–6 hubs, 3–4 mechanically distinct set-piece dilemmas, save point every 10–20 min, failed runs shorter with legible developing crisis.
- **Episodic verdict**: no evidence customers buy route legs; ship ONE complete voyage, then add destinations/regions as content (Caribbean Sail, Vagrus model). Banner Saga trilogy finished but financing strained ($2M for game 3) — not a solo template.
- **Art**: stylized > realistic for historical credibility (credibility comes from map/constraints/language/consequences); asset tier that works = route map + reusable ship view + few port compositions + portraits + event cards + icons; Sunless Sea warning: "text and pictures" still meant 415 illustrations/250k words/£250k/15 months. AI art: no reception evidence either way in this niche; Göktürk discloses it, unproven (7 reviews); one pro-AI curator ≠ acceptance.
- **Viking market**: not empty (Northgard, Expeditions: Viking 81%/1,956 reviews, Dead in Vinland 78%) but no successful direct equivalent of a destination-bound Norse merchant-crew survival journey. The niche is open AND the closest attempt died — both true.

### CM-style honest-invention / gap flags

- No telemetry-based death-rate data exists for any of these games
- HLTB figures are small self-reported samples (Caribbean Sail n=3 on main story)
- "49 products ≠ 49 designs" (Oregon Trail editions inflate count)
- Researcher's pacing targets explicitly hypotheses to playtest

## Perplexity Run 2 — AI research: feasibility, stack, art pipeline, saves, legal, costs (Nov 20, 2026)

## Perplexity Run 3 — Rus trade with Byzantium and the Islamic world, c. 750–950 AD (Nov 20, 2026)

Source: Andrzej's paste. **NOT deep research — GPT-6.2 Sol with Thinking, single-shot web-synthesized answer**, 187 cited links. Quality high: DOCUMENTED/RECONSTRUCTED/SPECULATIVE labels honored throughout, explicitly refuses defensible percentages, distinguishes "documented statement" from "verified event", uses Ibn Fadlan (922) and De Administrando Imperio (mid-10th c.) only with date caveats. ### Answers to the two questions
- **Skins or slaves? BOTH — and no defensible percentages for either.** Enslaved people: central, established, taxable on both routes (Ibn Rusta: Rus raid the Ṣaqāliba and sell them in Khazaria and Bulghar; Ibn Fadlan: enslaved women for sale, buyer visits quarters, Bulghar ruler's 1-in-10 right; DAI: captives in chains on the Dnieper portage; treaties: captive redemption clauses). Furs: equally major and organized (Ibn Khordadbeh: beaver + black fox pelts (and swords); Ibn Rusta/Ibn Fadlan's merchants: sable, squirrel; Birka archaeology: thousands of fox/pine-marten/squirrel paw bones = pelts finished at Birka on substantial scale). Scholarly state of play: Jankowiak argues captives were the main driver of the dirham influx; Howard-Johnston / Kovalev / Noonan build the organized fur-road model; "Viking-Age Trade: Silver, Slaves and Gotland" presents slavery as complementary explanation, not demonstrated replacement; Raffield: slaving prominent in texts, near-invisible archaeologically. Both "furs first" AND "primarily slave traders" are serious interpretations; neither admits percentages.
- **How many ships in one trading party? NO SOURCE ANSWERS IT.** Direct evidence of convoy organization exists without numbers: DAI ch. 9 has a merchant flotilla waiting 2–3 days at Vitichev for boats to assemble (no count, no crew table). Every hard number is war-fleet or legend: 907 chronicle 2,000 ships × 40 men (legendary campaign narrative); al-Masʿūdī ~500 ships × 100 men for the ~913 Caspian expedition; 941 accounts diverge 10,000 vs ~1,000 ships. The treaties' "fifty" = unarmed group admitted through the Constantinople gate, not convoy size. Ibn Fadlan's "ten or twenty" = occupants per HOUSE, not crew per hull. Report's explicit design guidance: **a small player party joining a larger, unnumbered flotilla is closer to the evidence than presenting 40–50 people per vessel as standard; any exact roster is SPECULATIVE.** Crew reference point: Skuldelev 1 (~16 m ocean trader, c. 1030) crew 6–8.

### 911/912-specific mechanics (game-usable)
- **Treaty precision — the three agreements are NOT interchangeable.** The commercial privileges (St. Mamas lodging + registration, six months' provisions, baths, unarmed entry 50 at a time with escort, duty-free, return ship supplies) belong to the 907 tradition and recur in 944/945 — NOT to the surviving 911 instrument, which is dated 2 Sept 911 and covers peace, restitution, stranded vessels, captives/fugitives, estates. Game may use the 907 clauses as a RECONSTRUCTION of Oleg's-peace trade terms; must not present Igor-era rules (ship-letter requirement, 50-bezant silk cap) as 911 law.
- **Cargo roster by evidence strength** (Volga core = sable + enslaved people, the strongest paired cargo): fox/squirrel/marten fur = strong (Birka paw-bone record); beaver/black fox = documented (incl. protein-analysis confirmation in Danish elite graves); wax + honey + a few swords = defensible secondary (chronicle gift-speeches corroborate the mix, not shares); ermine = attested only later (al-Muqaddasī), plausible cargo; amber = occasional, no regular allotment; walrus ivory and bulk timber = NOT established for 911 (Greenland-Kyiv ivory is later; Constantine's hull trade is within the Dnieper system).
- **Tolls — three distinct things, not one schedule** (feeds the toll-memory mechanic): Khazar 1/10 levy on the Caspian route (Ibn Khordadbeh) ≠ Bulghar ruler's 1-in-10 of enslaved people offered for sale (Ibn Fadlan) ≠ al-Masʿūdī's booty-share military bargain. Rulers take cuts; no single tariff list for 911/912.
- **Two overlapping systems, no grand circuit.** Dnieper/Kiev→Constantinople: regulated imperial market, silk = distinctive purchase, gold = payment/diplomatic/ransom (Byzantine coin finds nowhere near the dirham influx). Volga→Bulghar: dirhams from Central-Asia-connected intermediaries (incl. early Bulghar imitations) — a silver-seeking crew's better-grounded destination; Itil after the Khazar toll = further choice. **Baghdad = exceptional onward journey**: IF reached at all, by camel caravan from the southern Caspian (Ibn Khordadbeh's onwards-leg); no Rus party documented in Baghdad in 911/912; a Radhaniya contact is possible fiction. Crossover exists (Byzantine silk brocade on the Volga Rus funeral in Ibn Fadlan) but goods crossing ≠ one crew doing the circuit.
- **Season clock (DAI sequence, RECONSTRUCTED for 911/912):** winter tribute rounds among tributary communities → Kiev after thaw → June departure → Vitichev muster → seven rapids → at Aeifor/Neasit: guards posted, cargo unloaded, chained captives carried ~6 miles overland, boats dragged → Pecheneg warning at the Cherson ford. Player-legible portage and must mechanics fully grounded (for mid-10th c.; label it).
- **Dirham flow at the game's date: strong.** 9th-c. largely Abbasid → early-10th-c. renewed stream largely Samanid; sharp fall in fresh issues reaching Scandinavia ~954/955, marked decline ~970, weak final wave 990–1010. Spillings hoard ~67 kg / ~14,300 coins (latest coin 870/871); Kershaw: recycled Islamic silver in early Baltic objects → coin counts understate the influx. Mint name ≠ travel stamp of the last owner.

### Tension/decision flags for the concept one-pager
1. **Route-fork open call: research SUPPORTS the one-pager's structure.** Constantinople (from Kiev) and Bulghar = both established, well-attested destinations; Baghdad = possible but exceptional. A single crew doing both main branches in different seasons is plausible; a one-hull same-season grand circuit is not documented.

Knarr = Baltic leg model only; inland craft = lodya/monoxyla-type built/refitted hulls (Sorokin: clinker construction at Ladoga, local traditions alongside).
4. **913 solicitation event unaffected:** al-Masʿūdī's ~500 ships stands as DOCUMENTED-AS-CLAIM (report stresses even war-fleet figures diverge across sources — supports the game's dramatic-irony use, with provenance label).

## Run 6c — 9th-century Scandinavian (and Rus'-site) domestic architecture (Nov 21, 2026; art-blocking research after Andrzej flagged chimneys + non-longhouse cottages in scenes 14/15)

### The two verdicts, confirmed
1. **CHIMNEYS = ANACHRONISM, full stop.** Viking-Age houses had no chimneys and no glass windows; smoke escaped through a roof smoke hole or a gable wind-eye (vindauga, the source of the word "window"); interiors were smoky, and reconstructions show they were surprisingly bright when smoke holes were open ([National Museum of Denmark](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/viking-homes/), [Hurstwic](http://www.hurstwic.org/history/articles/daily_living/text/longhouse.htm)). Chimneys reached Scandinavian towns in the 1100s and rural dwellings only by the 1500s–1700s ([AskHistorians](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskHistorians/comments/4akzsc/in_film_we_see_depictions_of_viking_age_norse/)). ANY chimney stack in 9th-c. art (stone, brick or clay) = REJECT.
2. **The longhouse (langhús) is the dwelling archetype: long and low.** Typically 5–7 m wide and 15–75 m long (elite halls up to 83 m, Borg in Lofoten); walls slightly BOWED in plan like a ship's hull, wider midships; roof carried by two internal rows of posts (walls nearly non-load-bearing); wood frame on simple stone footings with walls of vertical planks set in the ground or clay-lined wattle; doors at the gable ends; roof low-pitched thick turf/sod (often grass-topped), birch-bark+sod, or shingles — never storybook-cottage gables. Central "long fire" (langeldar) hearth, stone-lined; benches/platforms along the walls; byre often under the same roof (cattle literally set the house's length — Journal of Danish Archaeology 9) ([Hurstwic](http://www.hurstwic.org/history/articles/daily_living/text/longhouse.htm), [Nationalmuseet](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/viking-homes/), [JDA vol 9](https://tidsskrift.dk/jda/article/download/127450/173733/269092)).
3. **Outbuildings:** farms = longhouse + byre/barn + semi-sunken pit-houses (grubehus) used as weaving/smithing workshops + storage buildings, inside a fence. Workshop smoke belongs to forges/sheds, not dwellings ([Valhalla Vikings](https://valhalla-vikings.co.uk/blogs/viking-history/viking-house)).
4. **East variant for our route (Ladoga and Rus' river towns):** earliest wooden structures from 753/754 (dendro), log construction — Rus traders' large timber structures housed 10–12 people (Ibn Fadlan); Ladoga houses were log-built craft dwellings, some with clay/stone dome ovens (pech-type) — documentable in the East, but still venting into the room, never through a chimney stack ([Encyclopedia.com: Staraya Ladoga](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/staraya-ladoga), [Global North/Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A050190A003274AB05E9A5B1190D1781/9781641894906c2_p37-52_CBO.pdf/archaeological-evidence-for-staraya-lagoda-as-an-early-scandinavian-emporium-of-the-global-north.pdf)).

### Art impact (style bible)
- Hard reject: chimney stacks of any material on any 9th-c. building. Correct smoke: thin wisps from roof ridges/gable wind-eyes.
- Correct silhouette in prompts: "long low oblong timber halls, gently bowed ship-like walls, gable-end doors, thick grass-topped turf roofs, no chimneys, no windows, thin smoke from roof-ridge openings" — avoid the words "cottage/fireplace/cabin" (they summon the wrong forms).
